## Part III: Problem-Solving (2 × 20 pts = 40 pts)

**PQ1.** [20 pts] **Inverse Dynamics Calculation**

During walking, the vertical GRF at the foot reaches a peak of 1.2 × BW = 820 N for a 70 kg person. The shank segment has mass m_shank = 3.0 kg, length L_shank = 0.40 m, and its COM is located at 43% of its length from the knee.

(a) [4 pts] Draw a free body diagram of the shank during the loading response (stance phase). Label all forces and moments.

(b) [6 pts] If the ankle moment at this instant is M_ankle = 30 N·m (plantarflexor moment), and the shank angular acceleration α = 2 rad/s², calculate the knee moment M_knee. (I_shank ≈ 0.003 kg·m²)

(c) [5 pts] If the GRF vector makes an angle of 15° with vertical, calculate the anterior-posterior component of GRF. What does this component represent biomechanically?

(d) [5 pts] Estimate the tibiofemoral joint reaction force if the knee moment is M_knee = 40 N·m and the quadriceps tendon inserts at 5 cm anterior to the knee joint center.

---

**PQ2.** [20 pts] **Fracture Risk Assessment and Design**

You are designing a bone screw for fracture fixation in the femoral shaft.

(a) [5 pts] The screw shaft has diameter d = 4 mm. Under tensile pull-out force F = 500 N, what is the tensile stress σ in the screw shaft?

(b) [5 pts] If the screw threads strip the bone with shear stress τ_s = 30 MPa, and the thread outer diameter D = 5 mm, what pull-out force causes stripping? (Hint: approximate thread surface area = π × D × thread_depth × n_threads)

(c) [5 pts] The cortical bone around the screw has E = 18 GPa and σ_yield = 120 MPa. Using the stress concentration factor at the screw head (K_t ≈ 2.0), what is the maximum stress at the head? What is the safety factor?

(d) [5 pts] An alternative design uses a 6 mm diameter screw. If the pull-out force remains 500 N, how does the stress compare? By what factor does the cross-sectional area increase?

## Answer Key

### MCQ Answers
| Q | Answer | Key Point |
|---|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **C** (20%) | Two double-support periods × ~10% each = ~20% |
| 2 | **A** | Measure GRF (force plate) → compute joint angles (markers) → inverse dynamics → joint torques |
| 3 | **C** (1.1× BW) | First peak ≈ 1.0-1.2 × BW |
| 4 | **B** (1.3 m/s) | v = stride_length × cadence / 120 = 1.4 × 110 / 120 = 1.28 m/s |
| 5 | **B** | Inverted pendulum exchange between KE and PE |
| 6 | **B** (πd³/32) | I = πd⁴/64, c = d/2, Z = I/c = πd³/32 |
| 7 | **D** (62%) | Toe-off = end of stance = beginning of swing = ~62% |
| 8 | **C** | Spiral fracture = torsion + tension on oblique plane |
| 9 | **B** | Iα = rotational inertia × angular acceleration |
| 10 | **C** (2-3× BW) | Running GRF peaks reach 2-3× BW |

### Short Answer Solutions

**SQ1**: Loading response = 0-12% of gait cycle. Key event: first double support begins at heel strike. GRF rises to first peak. Knee flexes slightly (~15°) to absorb shock. This is the only time both feet are on the ground in early stance.

**SQ2**: (a) ω_avg = 60° × π/180 / 0.2 s = 1.047/0.2 = 5.24 rad/s. (b) Assuming constant acceleration, ω_f = 2 × ω_avg = 10.47 rad/s. (c) α = (ω_f - ω_0)/t = 10.47/0.2 = 52.4 rad/s².

**SQ3**: Six determinants: (1) Pelvic rotation ±4° — reduces COM rise; (2) Pelvic tilt ±2°; (3) Knee flexion in stance; (4) Ankle dorsiflexion; (5) Lateral pelvic shift; (6) Foot mechanics. Pelvic rotation: as pelvis rotates forward ~4° each side, it drops COM by ~0.5 cm, partially offsetting the rise from leg length.

**SQ4**: M_max = FL/4 = 500 × 0.2/4 = 25 N·m. σ_max = Mc/I = 25 × 0.015 / (2.5×10⁻⁸) = 15 MPa.

**SQ5**: OA patients avoid knee flexion during swing because: (1) Pain: flexion increases tibiofemoral contact stress (σ = F/A; smaller contact area → higher stress); (2) Quadriceps weakness: insufficient extensor moment to control knee during swing; (3) Joint stiffness: osteophyte formation restricts range of motion; (4) Stability: reduced flexion decreases risk of buckling/instability.

### Problem Solutions

**PQ1**: (b) ΣM_knee = I·α: M_knee = M_ankle + I_shank·α = 30 + 0.003×2 = 30.006 N·m ≈ 30 N·m. (c) F_ap = 820 × sin(15°) = 820 × 0.259 = 212 N (braking force). (d) Quadriceps force F_q = M_knee/0.05 = 40/0.05 = 800 N. Knee joint reaction R_knee = F_q + body weight component ≈ 800 + 700 = 1500 N ≈ 2.2× BW.

**PQ2**: (a) A = π(2mm)² = 12.57 mm²; σ = 500/12.57 = 39.8 MPa. (b) Area ≈ π×5mm×0.5mm×5 = 39.3 mm²; F_strip = 30 MPa × 39.3 = 1179 N. (c) σ_max = 2×39.8 = 79.6 MPa; SF = 120/79.6 = 1.51. (d) Area increase: A_new/A_old = (6/4)² = 2.25×.
