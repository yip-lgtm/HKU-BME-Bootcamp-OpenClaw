# Week 16 Readings — Biotransport (Diffusion and Fluid Mechanics)

## Required Readings

### Primary Textbook
- **Truskey, G.A., Yuan, F., & Katz, D.F.** (2009) *Transport Phenomena in Biological Systems*, 2nd ed. — Prentice Hall. ISBN: 978-0131569881

  - Chapters 1-4: Blood rheology; Poiseuille flow; microcirculation mechanics
  - Chapters 5-7: Starling's hypothesis; transcapillary exchange; interstitial fluid flow
  - Chapters 8-10: Vascular mechanics; wave propagation;心脏血流动力学

### Key Papers
1. **Krogh, A.** (1922) "The number and distribution of capillaries in muscles with calculations of the oxygen pressure head necessary for supplying the tissue" — *Journal of Physiology*, 52(6), 409-415. (Foundational capillary-tissue exchange model)
2. **Teorell, T.** (1953) "Transport processes and electrical phenomena in ionic membranes" — *Progress in Biophysics and Biophysical Chemistry*, 3, 305-369. (Membrane transport)
3. **Starling, E.H.** (1896) "On the absorption of fluids from the connective tissue spaces" — *Journal of Physiology*, 19, 312-326. (Starling's hypothesis)

5. **Michel, C.C. & Curry, F.E.** (1999) "Microvascular permeability" — *Physiological Reviews*, 79(3), 703-761.

## Learning Objectives (BMED4603)

By the end of Week 16, students should be able to:
1. State and apply Fick's 1st and 2nd laws of diffusion to calculate solute flux and concentration profiles
2. Derive and apply Starling's equation for net transcapillary fluid exchange
3. Explain the Kedem-Katchalsky formulation for coupled solute-solvent transport across membranes
4. Apply the Krogh cylinder model to calculate tissue oxygen tension and capillary radius
5. Distinguish between diffusive and convective transport regimes using the Peclet number
6. Solve simple problems in 1-D steady-state and transient diffusion
7. Understand the Navier-Stokes equations in simplified form for biological tubes
8. Apply Poiseuille's law to calculate blood flow resistance in vessels
