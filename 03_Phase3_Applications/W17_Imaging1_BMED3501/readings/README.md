# Week 17 Readings — Medical Imaging I (X-ray, CT, Ultrasound)

## Required Readings

### Primary Textbook
- **Bushberg, J.T., Seibert, J.A., Leidholdt, E.M. & Boone, J.M.** (2012) *The Essential Physics of Medical Imaging*, 3rd ed. — Lippincott Williams & Wilkins. ISBN: 978-0781786108
  - Chapters 1-4: Radiation physics; X-ray production; interaction of X-rays with matter
  - Chapters 5-7: Film-screen imaging; digital radiography; computed tomography
  - Chapters 8-9: Ultrasound physics; Doppler imaging
  - Chapters 10-11: Image quality; noise and artifacts

- **Prince, J.L. & Links, J.M.** (2014) *Medical Imaging Signals and Systems*, 2nd ed. — Pearson. ISBN: 978-0132148889
  - Chapters 3-4: Fourier transform; linear systems; convolution
  - Chapters 5-6: X-ray imaging; computed tomography reconstruction
  - Chapters 9-10: Ultrasound imaging; acoustic wave propagation

### Key Papers

3. **Wild, J.J. & Reid, J.M.** (1952) "The use of pulsed ultrasound to obtain images" — *Electronics*, 25, 174-176. (First clinical ultrasound)
4. **Kremkau, F.W.** (2006) *Diagnostic Ultrasound: Principles and Instruments*, 7th ed. — Elsevier. (Comprehensive ultrasound text)

## Learning Objectives (BMED3501)

By the end of Week 17, students should be able to:
1. Explain X-ray production (bremsstrahlung, characteristic radiation) and the factors affecting X-ray beam quality
2. Apply the Lambert-Beer law to calculate X-ray attenuation through materials
3. Describe CT image reconstruction using filtered back projection (FBP) and iterative methods
4. Calculate Hounsfield units and explain their relationship to tissue attenuation
5. Explain the physics of ultrasound (wave propagation, acoustic impedance, reflection/transmission)
6. Apply the Doppler equation to calculate blood flow velocity from frequency shift
7. Compare the advantages and limitations of X-ray/CT vs. ultrasound imaging
8. Define key image quality metrics: spatial resolution, contrast, noise, MTF
