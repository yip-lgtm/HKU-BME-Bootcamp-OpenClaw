# Week 18 Readings — Medical Imaging II (MRI, Nuclear, and Optical Imaging)

## Required Readings

### Primary Textbook
- **McRobbie, D.W., Moore, E.A., Graves, M.J., & Prince, M.R.** (2017) *MRI from Picture to Proton*, 3rd ed. — Cambridge University Press. ISBN: 978-1107707025
  - Chapters 1-4: MRI physics; Larmor precession; RF pulses; Bloch equations
  - Chapters 5-8: Relaxation mechanisms; T1, T2, T2*; image contrast
  - Chapters 9-12: K-space; image encoding; pulse sequences
  - Chapters 13-15: Hardware; artifacts; advanced techniques

- **Cherry, S.R., Sorenson, J.A., & Phelps, M.E.** (2012) *Physics in Nuclear Medicine*, 4th ed. — Elsevier/Saunders. ISBN: 978-1451118381
  - Chapters 1-4: Radioactivity; decay modes; radiopharmaceuticals
  - Chapters 5-8: Gamma camera; PET; SPECT physics
  - Chapters 9-12: Image reconstruction; quantitative imaging; SUV

### Key Papers
1. **Lauterbur, P.C.** (1973) "Image formation by induced local interactions: Examples employing nuclear magnetic resonance" — *Nature*, 242, 190-191. (Nobel Prize 2003 — MRI invention)
2. **Mansfield, P.** (1977) "Multi-planar image formation using NMR spin echoes" — *Journal of Physics C*, 10, L55-L58. (Echo planar imaging)
3. **Phelps, M.E. et al.** (1975) "Application of annihilation coincidence detection to transaxial reconstruction tomography" — *Journal of Nuclear Medicine*, 16, 210-224. (PET principles)
4. **Huang, D. et al.** (1991) "Optical coherence tomography" — *Science*, 254(5035), 1178-1181. (OCT invention)

## Learning Objectives (BMED3501/BMED3504)

By the end of Week 18, students should be able to:
1. Explain Larmor precession and the physical basis of MRI signal generation
2. Describe T1 and T2 relaxation mechanisms and their contribution to image contrast
3. Explain k-space encoding and how Fourier transforms produce MRI images
4. Compare PET and SPECT imaging principles including coincidence detection
5. Calculate standardized uptake value (SUV) for PET quantification
6. Describe optical imaging modalities: OCT, fluorescence, near-infrared imaging
7. Compare the sensitivity, resolution, and clinical applications of MRI, PET, and optical imaging
8. Discuss molecular imaging agents and their targeting mechanisms
