## Answer Key

### MCQ Answers

| Q | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | **B** | 50-200 nm is the sweet spot for EPR: small enough to escape RES, large enough to not clear through kidneys |
| 2 | **C** | PNIPAAm LCST ≈ 32°C (close to body temperature, useful for thermoresponsive applications) |
| 3 | **C** | Steric repulsion from hydrated PEG chains physically prevents protein adsorption |
| 4 | **A** | Glycolic acid units are more hydrophilic → faster water penetration → faster hydrolysis |
| 5 | **B** | 10-20 nm spacing is optimal; < 5 nm is unnecessary, > 70 nm loses adhesion |
| 6 | **B** | SLA (sandblasted + acid-etched) creates micro-nano roughness for optimal osseointegration |
| 7 | **B** | Soft materials (< 1 kPa) promote M2 through mechanotransduction pathways |
| 8 | **C** | PEGylation increases circulation half-life from minutes to hours, enabling tumor accumulation |
| 9 | **B** | Shear-thinning allows easy extrusion through nozzle while maintaining shape after deposition |
| 10 | **B** | Magnesium is an essential ion, promotes endothelial healing, and the stent degrades at controlled rate |

### Short Answer Solutions

**SQ1**: Biodegradable = materials that degrade via chemical processes (hydrolysis, enzyme action). Bioresorbable = materials that degrade AND are absorbed/metabolized by the body. Example: PLGA (biodegradable), magnesium (bioresorbable).

**SQ2**: Timeline: seconds (protein adsorption), hours-days (neutrophils/macrophages), weeks (granulation tissue), months (fibrous capsule). Key cells: neutrophils (acute), macrophages (chronic), fibroblasts (granulation), myofibroblasts (capsule contraction).

**SQ3**: Poly(β-amino ester) (PbAE) or poly(acrylic acid) (PAA). These polymers have amine groups that protonate at acidic pH, causing charge-mediated swelling and drug release. At pH 7.4, the polymer is neutral/hydrophobic.

**SQ4**: E_bone ≈ 18 GPa, E_Ti ≈ 110 GPa (6× mismatch). This causes stress shielding → bone resorption. Solutions: add porosity, use less stiff alloys (β-Ti), use coating with intermediate stiffness.

**SQ5**: RGD binds integrin receptors (α5β1, αvβ3, αIIbβ3) through RGD sequence recognition. This activates focal adhesion kinase (FAK) → downstream signaling (Src, paxillin) → cytoskeletal reorganization → cell spreading.

### Problem Solutions

**PQ1**:
(a) h ≈ 0.5 × √5000 = 0.5 × 70.7 = 35.4 nm (both sides = ~71 nm total)
(b) 80 nm + 2×35 nm = 150 nm total
(c) Using empirical model: t½ ≈ 10 × (1 + 0.1×5000) × (1 + 0.005×150000) / 60 ≈ 15 hours (>6 hours ✓)
(d) With 20 kDa: h ≈ 0.5 × √20000 = 70.7 nm, t½ increases significantly. Trade-off: larger PEG = longer circulation BUT may reduce drug release rate and cell uptake.
(e) Active targeting ligands (RGD, folate, antibodies), pH-sensitive linkers, magnetic targeting, focused ultrasound.

**PQ2**:
(a) 5 μg / 0.08 = 62.5 μg PLGA
(b) k ≈ 0.02/day for 50:50. t_half = ln(2)/k ≈ 35 days. MW < 10 kDa at t ≈ ln(30000/10000)/0.02 ≈ 55 days.
(c) M(t) = 0.30×(1 - e^(-1.0×t)) + 0.70×(1 - e^(-0.08×t))
(d) Day 1: 0.26 = 26%; Day 7: 0.56 = 56%; Day 21: 0.88 = 88%
(e) ELISA for BMP-2 concentration, BCA assay for protein, HPLC for quantification, bioassay (alkaline phosphatase for osteogenic activity).
