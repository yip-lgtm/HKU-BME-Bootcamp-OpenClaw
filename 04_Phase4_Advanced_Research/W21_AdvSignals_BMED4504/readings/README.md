# Week 21 Readings — Advanced Biomedical Signals (BMED4504)

## Required Readings

### Primary Literature

1. **Daubechies, I.** (1990) *Ten Lectures on Wavelets* — SIAM. ISBN: 978-0898712742
   - The foundational wavelet textbook. Focus on Ch. 1-4 (introduction, Fourier transform limitations, continuous wavelet transform).

2. **Hyvärinen, A. & Oja, E.** (2000) "Independent Component Analysis: Algorithms and Applications" — *Neural Networks*, 13(4-5), 411-430.
   - The ICA algorithm paper. Introduces FastICA, Infomax.

3. **Haykin, S.** (2001) *Adaptive Filter Theory*, 4th ed. — Prentice Hall. ISBN: 978-0130901262
   - Chapters 4-6: LMS algorithm, RLS algorithm, frequency-domain adaptive filters.

4. **Candes, E.J. & Wakin, M.B.** (2008) "An Introduction to Compressive Sampling" — *IEEE Signal Processing Magazine*, 25(2), 21-30.
   - The compressive sensing revolution paper.

### Key Papers

1. **Mallat, S.G. & Zhang, Z.** (1993) "Matching pursuits with time-frequency dictionaries" — *IEEE Trans. Signal Processing*, 41(12), 3397-3415.
2. **Bell, A.J. & Sejnowski, T.J.** (1995) "An information-maximization approach to blind separation and blind deconvolution" — *Neural Computation*, 7(6), 1129-1159.
3. **Donoho, D.L.** (2006) "Compressed sensing" — *IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory*, 52(4), 1289-1306.

### Learning Objectives (BMED4504)

By the end of Week 21, students should be able to:

1. Apply wavelet transforms (CWT and DWT) for time-frequency analysis of biomedical signals
2. Implement ICA for blind source separation (EEG artifact removal)
3. Design LMS and RLS adaptive filters for noise cancellation
4. Understand compressive sensing and its application to biomedical imaging
5. Compare time-frequency representations for non-stationary signals
