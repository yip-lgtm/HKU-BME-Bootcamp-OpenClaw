# Week 3 Self-Quiz — Bioenergetics, Enzymes & Metabolism

## Multiple Choice (15 questions)

**1. The standard free energy change for ATP hydrolysis (ATP → ADP + Pi) is:**
- A. -7.3 kJ/mol
- B. -30.5 kJ/mol
- C. -50 kJ/mol
- D. -100 kJ/mol
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: ΔG°' = -30.5 kJ/mol. However, in the cell where [ATP] >> [ADP], ΔGcell ≈ -50 to -60 kJ/mol.

**2. Which enzyme is the rate-limiting step of glycolysis?**
- A. Hexokinase
- B. Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)
- C. Pyruvate kinase
- D. Aldolase
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: PFK-1 is the committed step and major regulatory point of glycolysis. It is allosterically regulated by ATP (inhibitor) and AMP/F2,6BP (activators).

**3. The chemiosmotic hypothesis was proposed by:**
- A. Hans Krebs
- B. Peter Mitchell
- C. Paul Boyer
- D. Frederick Sanger
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Peter Mitchell (1961) proposed the chemiosmotic hypothesis, suggesting that ATP synthesis is driven by a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane. He received the Nobel Prize in 1978.

**4. How many ATP molecules are produced per molecule of glucose during aerobic respiration (using modern P/O ratios)?**
- A. 36-38
- B. 30-32
- C. 24-26
- D. 42-44
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Using modern estimates (NADH = 2.5 ATP, FADH2 = 1.5 ATP), the total is 30-32 ATP per glucose.

**5. Which of the following is NOT produced in the citric acid cycle?**
- A. NADH
- B. FADH2
- C. CO2
- D. O2
- **Answer**: D | **Explanation**: The citric acid cycle produces 3 NADH, 1 FADH2, and 2 CO2 per acetyl-CoA. O2 is the terminal electron acceptor in the electron transport chain, not in the CAC.

**6. Cyanide is lethal because it:**
- A. Inhibits Complex I of the electron transport chain
- B. Inhibits Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase)
- C. Uncouples oxidative phosphorylation
- D. Inhibits ATP synthase
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Cyanide binds to the ferric iron (Fe3+) of cytochrome a3 in Complex IV, preventing oxygen binding and blocking electron transfer to O2. This rapidly halts ATP synthesis.

**7. Which complex of the electron transport chain does NOT pump protons?**
- A. Complex I
- B. Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase)
- C. Complex III
- D. Complex IV
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase) transfers electrons from FADH2 to CoQ but does not pump protons because it bypasses the first three coupling sites.

**8. The regulatory enzyme most sensitive to cellular energy charge is:**
- A. Hexokinase
- B. Phosphofructokinase-1
- C. Pyruvate dehydrogenase
- D. Citrate synthase
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: PFK-1 is highly sensitive to ATP/AMP ratio, serving as the primary energy sensor. High ATP inhibits, while AMP activates.

**9. Which of the following statements about F2,6BP is correct?**
- A. It inhibits PFK-1
- B. It is synthesized by phosphofructokinase-2 (PFK-2)
- C. It is only found in muscle
- D. It decreases glycolytic flux
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: F2,6BP is synthesized by the bifunctional enzyme PFK-2 (which has kinase and phosphatase activities). It is the most potent activator of PFK-1.

**10. In beta-oxidation of fatty acids, each cycle produces:**
- A. 1 acetyl-CoA and 1 NADH
- B. 1 acetyl-CoA, 1 FADH2, and 1 NADH
- C. 2 acetyl-CoA and 1 NADH
- D. 1 acetyl-CoA only
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Each round of beta-oxidation produces: 1 acetyl-CoA, 1 FADH2 (from the first dehydrogenation), and 1 NADH (from the second dehydrogenation).

**11. The enzyme that catalyzes the first step of the citric acid cycle is:**
- A. Isocitrate dehydrogenase
- B. Citrate synthase
- C. Aconitase
- D. Alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Citrate synthase catalyzes the condensation of acetyl-CoA with oxaloacetate to form citrate. This is the first committed step of the CAC.

**12. Which of the following is an example of substrate-level phosphorylation?**
- A. ATP synthesis by ATP synthase
- B. Photophosphorylation in chloroplasts
- C. Phosphoglycerate kinase reaction in glycolysis
- D. Pyruvate dehydrogenase reaction
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Phosphoglycerate kinase transfers a phosphate from 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate to ADP, directly generating ATP without involving the electron transport chain.

**13. The Pasteur effect refers to:**
- A. Increased glycolysis in the presence of oxygen
- B. Decreased glycolysis in the presence of oxygen
- C. Lactate production under anaerobic conditions
- D. ATP production from fatty acids
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The Pasteur effect describes how oxygen inhibits glycolysis (and lactate production) in yeast and other organisms. This is because high ATP and citrate from oxidative phosphorylation inhibit PFK-1.

**14. Which statement about uncouplers is correct?**
- A. They inhibit ATP synthase
- B. They prevent proton gradient formation
- C. They increase ATP synthesis
- D. They bind to cytochrome c oxidase
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Uncouplers (like DNP) dissipate the proton gradient by carrying protons across the inner membrane, allowing electron transport to continue but preventing ATP synthesis. This converts energy to heat.

**15. Brown adipose tissue generates heat through:**
- A. Increased glycolysis
- B. UCP1 (uncoupling protein 1) mediated proton leakage
- C. Enhanced fatty acid synthesis
- D. Increased lactate production
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: UCP1 (thermogenin) in brown fat uncouples oxidative phosphorylation, allowing the proton gradient to be dissipated as heat rather than used for ATP synthesis. This is crucial for non-shivering thermogenesis.

## Short Answer Questions

### Question 1 (10 marks)
**Explain why cyanide causes rapid death whereas rotenone poisoning has a slower onset.**

**Model Answer**:
- Cyanide (Complex IV inhibitor):
  - Blocks the final step of electron transport
  - Prevents O2 from accepting electrons
  - Rapid ATP depletion in tissues with high O2 demand (brain, heart)
  - CNS failure within minutes
  - No ATP for Na+/K+-ATPase → depolarization → death

- Rotenone (Complex I inhibitor):
  - Blocks earlier in ETC
  - Electrons can still flow partially through Complex II
  - Compensatory mechanisms may initially operate
  - Slower accumulation of NADH and metabolic stress
  - Death may occur over hours to days

### Question 2 (10 marks)
**Calculate the ATP yield from oxidation of one molecule of stearic acid (C18:0).**

**Model Answer**:
- Stearic acid: 18 carbons
- Activation: -2 ATP
- β-oxidation cycles: 18/2 - 1 = 8 cycles
- Products:
  - 9 acetyl-CoA → 9 × 10 ATP = 90 ATP
  - 8 NADH → 8 × 2.5 = 20 ATP
  - 8 FADH2 → 8 × 1.5 = 12 ATP
- Total: 90 + 20 + 12 - 2 = 120 ATP
- Per carbon: 120/18 = 6.7 ATP (vs. glucose ~5.3 ATP)

### Question 3 (10 marks)
**Describe how AMPK (AMP-activated protein kinase) senses cellular energy status and activates catabolic pathways.**

**Model Answer**:
- AMPK senses:
  - ↑ AMP/ATP ratio (energy depletion)
  - ↑ ADP/ATP ratio
  
- Activation mechanism:
  - AMP binds to AMPK γ subunit
  - Promotes phosphorylation by LKB1
  - Inhibits PP2C phosphatases
  
- Metabolic effects:
  - Activates: GLUT4 translocation, glycolysis (PFK-2), fatty acid oxidation
  - Inhibits: Lipogenesis, glycogen synthesis, protein synthesis
  
- Overall: Restores energy balance by promoting catabolism
