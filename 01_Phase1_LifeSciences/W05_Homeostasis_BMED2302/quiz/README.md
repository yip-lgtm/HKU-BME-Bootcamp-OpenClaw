### Question 1 (6 points)
Which of the following best describes the concept of homeostasis?

A. The body's cells maintain exactly the same internal environment regardless of external changes
B. The body actively maintains physiological variables within a narrow, specific range
C. All physiological variables are held at constant values at all times
D. Homeostasis only applies to temperature regulation

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Homeostasis (defined by Walter Cannon, 1932) refers to the body's ability to maintain internal stability by actively regulating physiological variables within narrow ranges. This is dynamic, not static—variables fluctuate but are kept within bounds.

### Question 2 (6 points)
During an action potential, the rapid depolarization phase is primarily caused by:

A. K⁺ efflux
B. Na⁺ influx
C. Ca²⁺ release
D. Cl⁻ influx

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: During the upstroke of an action potential, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open rapidly, allowing Na⁺ to flow down its electrochemical gradient into the cell, causing depolarization. This Na⁺ influx is the primary driver of the rapid depolarization phase (Hodgkin & Huxley, 1952).

### Question 3 (6 points)
Which phase of the cardiac action potential is responsible for the long refractory period?

A. Phase 0 (rapid depolarization)
B. Phase 1 (early repolarization)
C. Phase 2 (plateau)
D. Phase 3 (repolarization)

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: The plateau phase (Phase 2) is unique to cardiac muscle and is maintained by L-type Ca²⁺ channels. This prolonged depolarization, lasting ~200 ms, creates the long refractory period that prevents tetanic contraction and ensures efficient pumping.

### Question 4 (6 points)
Which of the following correctly describes the absolute refractory period?

A. It is possible to generate another action potential if the stimulus is strong enough
B. No action potential can be generated regardless of stimulus strength
C. The Na⁺/K⁺ pump is temporarily inactivated
D. The membrane potential is more positive than at rest

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: During the absolute refractory period, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are in the inactivated state and cannot be opened again regardless of stimulus strength. This prevents backward propagation of the action potential and limits maximum firing frequency.

### Question 5 (6 points)
In the blood glucose regulation system, insulin primarily acts to:

A. Stimulate glycogenolysis in the liver
B. Promote glucose uptake into muscle and adipose tissue
C. Inhibit insulin secretion from β cells
D. Stimulate gluconeogenesis

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Insulin promotes glucose uptake by stimulating translocation of GLUT4 transporters to cell membranes in muscle and adipose tissue. It also inhibits hepatic glucose production (gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis) and promotes glycogen synthesis.

### Question 6 (6 points)
Which hormone is released from the adrenal medulla during the fight-or-flight response?

A. Cortisol
B. Aldosterone
C. Epinephrine (adrenaline)
D. Testosterone

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: The adrenal medulla releases catecholamines—primarily epinephrine (80%) and norepinephrine (20%)—during sympathetic activation. These hormones increase heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose levels to prepare the body for immediate action.

### Question 7 (6 points)
The hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis is an example of:

A. Positive feedback regulation
B. Negative feedback with hormone cascades
C. Direct neural control
D. Autocrine signaling

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The HPT axis operates through a classic hormone cascade with negative feedback. TRH → TSH → Thyroid hormones (T3/T4) → Negative feedback inhibition of both hypothalamus and pituitary. This ensures precise regulation of metabolic rate.

### Question 8 (6 points)
In the Goldman equation, if the membrane becomes equally permeable to Na⁺ and K⁺, the membrane potential would be approximately:

A. -90 mV (K⁺ equilibrium potential)
B. 0 mV
C. +60 mV (Na⁺ equilibrium potential)
D. -70 mV (typical resting potential)

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The Goldman equation calculates Vm as a weighted average of ionic equilibrium potentials. If PK⁺ = PNa⁺, and since [K⁺]in + [Na⁺]in ≈ [K⁺]out + [Na⁺]out, the numerator and denominator become nearly equal, resulting in Vm ≈ 0 mV.

### Question 9 (6 points)
Which of the following is the correct sequence of events in an action potential?

A. Repolarization → Hyperpolarization → Resting potential → Depolarization
B. Depolarization → Repolarization → Hyperpolarization → Resting potential
C. Resting potential → Hyperpolarization → Depolarization → Repolarization
D. Depolarization → Hyperpolarization → Repolarization → Resting potential

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The correct sequence is: Resting potential → Depolarization (Na⁺ influx) → Repolarization (K⁺ efflux) → Hyperpolarization (excess K⁺ efflux) → Return to resting potential. The hyperpolarization occurs because K⁺ channels close slowly, allowing additional K⁺ efflux beyond the resting potential.

### Question 10 (6 points)
In Type 2 diabetes mellitus, the primary defect is:

A. Complete loss of β cells
B. Autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing cells
C. Insulin resistance combined with progressive β-cell dysfunction
D. Defective insulin receptors

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Type 2 DM is characterized by insulin resistance (cells don't respond properly to insulin) followed by β-cell dysfunction (β cells can't produce enough insulin to compensate). This is different from Type 1 (autoimmune β-cell destruction) and MODY (genetic β-cell defects).

## Part B: Short Answer Questions (40 points)

### Question 1 (8 points)
**Compare and contrast negative feedback and positive feedback mechanisms in physiology. Provide one specific example of each and explain why negative feedback is more common in biological systems.**

**Sample Answer**:

**Negative Feedback**:
- Definition: Response counteracts the initial stimulus
- Properties: Maintains stability, self-correcting, oscillations possible
- Example: Body temperature regulation
  - If T drops below 37°C → shivering, vasoconstriction → T rises → response stops
- Prevalence: Most common (90%+ of physiological systems)

**Positive Feedback**:
- Definition: Response amplifies the initial stimulus
- Properties: Destabilizing, leads to endpoint, can be harmful if uncontrolled
- Example: Childbirth (oxytocin cascade)
  - Baby's head pushes cervix → oxytocin release → contractions → more pressure → more oxytocin
  - Terminates when baby is delivered

**Why negative feedback dominates**:
1. **Stability**: Negative feedback maintains physiological variables within narrow ranges
2. **Safety**: Uncontrolled positive feedback can be life-threatening (e.g., uncontrolled hemorrhage → more bleeding)
3. **Adaptation**: Allows organisms to respond to changing conditions while maintaining internal constancy

### Question 2 (8 points)
**Using the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz voltage equation, calculate the resting membrane potential given the following ion concentrations and permeabilities. Show your work.**

Given:
- [K⁺]in = 150 mM, [K⁺]out = 5 mM, PK⁺ = 1
- [Na⁺]in = 15 mM, [Na⁺]out = 150 mM, PNa⁺ = 0.04
- [Cl⁻]in = 10 mM, [Cl⁻]out = 120 mM, PCl⁻ = 0.45
- Temperature = 37°C (310 K)

**Sample Answer**:

```
Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz Equation:
Vm = (RT/F) × ln[(PK[K⁺]out + PNa[Na⁺]out + PCl[Cl⁻]in) / 
                    (PK[K⁺]in + PNa[Na⁺]in + PCl[Cl⁻]out)]

RT/F at 37°C = (8.314 × 310) / 96485 = 0.0267 V = 26.7 mV

Numerator = (1 × 5) + (0.04 × 150) + (0.45 × 10)
          = 5 + 6 + 4.5 = 15.5

Denominator = (1 × 150) + (0.04 × 15) + (0.45 × 120)
            = 150 + 0.6 + 54 = 204.6

Vm = 26.7 × ln(15.5 / 204.6)
   = 26.7 × ln(0.0758)
   = 26.7 × (-2.58)
   = -68.8 mV

Answer: Resting membrane potential ≈ -69 mV

This is close to the typical value of -70 mV because K⁺ permeability 
dominates (PK/PNa = 25:1).
```

### Question 3 (8 points)
**Explain the pathophysiology of Type 1 versus Type 2 diabetes mellitus, including the role of β cells, insulin resistance, and the resulting metabolic effects.**

**Sample Answer**:

**Type 1 Diabetes**:
- Cause: Autoimmune destruction of β cells (T-cell mediated)
- Onset: Usually childhood/adolescence (but can occur at any age)
- β cells: >90% destroyed at diagnosis
- Insulin: Absolute deficiency
- Metabolic effects:
  - No insulin → ↑ blood glucose (can't enter cells)
  - Catabolism: ↑ glycogenolysis, ↑ gluconeogenesis, ↑ lipolysis
  - Weight loss despite eating
  - DKA risk (ketone production)

**Type 2 Diabetes**:
- Cause: Insulin resistance + β-cell dysfunction
- Onset: Usually adulthood (increasingly in younger populations)
- β cells: Initially try to compensate with hyperinsulinemia, then fail
- Insulin: Relative deficiency (not absent)
- Metabolic effects:
  - Insulin resistance → ↓ glucose uptake despite high insulin
  - Hyperglycemia despite hyperinsulinemia
  - Hyperinsulinemia → cardiovascular complications
  - HHS risk (hyperosmolar state, usually in elderly)

**Key Difference**: Type 1 = No insulin production; Type 2 = Normal/high insulin but cells don't respond.

### Question 4 (8 points)
**Describe the sequence of events during the absolute and relative refractory periods of an action potential, and explain their physiological significance.**

**Sample Answer**:

**Absolute Refractory Period (ARP)**:
- Duration: ~1 ms (neurons), ~200 ms (cardiac muscle)
- Mechanism: Na⁺ channels are in inactivated state (cannot reopen)
- Result: No action potential can be generated regardless of stimulus strength
- Purpose: Prevents backward propagation; ensures unidirectional conduction

**Relative Refractory Period (RRP)**:
- Duration: ~2-4 ms (neurons)
- Mechanism: 
  - Some Na⁺ channels recovering from inactivation
  - K⁺ channels still open (delayed rectifier)
- Result: Only suprathreshold stimuli can generate AP
- Purpose: Limits maximum firing frequency; prevents tetanic contraction in heart

**Physiological Significance**:
1. **Unidirectional propagation**: AP can only travel forward
2. **Maximum frequency encoding**: f_max ≈ 1/(ARP + RRP)
3. **Safety margin**: High-intensity stimuli don't cause multiple APs
4. **Cardiac protection**: Long ARP in heart prevents dangerous arrhythmias
