## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions (80 points)

### Chemistry (Questions 1-4)

### Question 1 (4 points)
The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation is used to calculate:
A. Enzyme reaction rates
B. The pH of a buffer solution
C. The equilibrium constant
D. The rate of diffusion

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation (pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])) is specifically used to calculate the pH of buffer solutions and to understand the relationship between pH and the ratio of acid to conjugate base.

---

### Question 2 (4 points)
Which of the following is NOT a weak acid commonly found in biological systems?
A. Carbonic acid (pKa = 6.1)
B. Lactic acid (pKa = 3.9)
C. Hydrochloric acid (pKa = -7)
D. Acetic acid (pKa = 4.8)

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is a strong acid (pKa = -7), meaning it dissociates completely in water. Weak acids (carbonic, lactic, acetic) only partially dissociate, making them useful for buffering.

---

### Question 3 (4 points)
In a solution with pH 7.4, the ratio of [A⁻]/[HA] for an acid with pKa = 6.1 is approximately:
A. 0.2
B. 2
C. 10
D. 20

**Answer**: D | **Explanation**: Using Henderson-Hasselbalch: log([A⁻]/[HA]) = pH - pKa = 7.4 - 6.1 = 1.3. Therefore [A⁻]/[HA] = 10^1.3 ≈ 20.

---

### Question 4 (4 points)
The high surface tension of water is primarily due to:
A. Covalent bonds between water molecules
B. Hydrogen bonds between water molecules
C. Ionic bonds in water
D. Van der Waals forces

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Water molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other, creating strong cohesive forces that result in high surface tension. This property is essential for capillary action, leaf water transport in plants, and many biological processes.

### Biomolecules (Questions 5-8)

### Question 5 (4 points)
The Michaelis-Menten equation describes:
A. The rate of diffusion across membranes
B. The rate of enzyme-catalyzed reactions as a function of substrate concentration
C. The equilibrium constant for reversible reactions
D. The rate of protein synthesis

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The Michaelis-Menten equation (v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S])) describes how the rate of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction depends on substrate concentration. Km is the substrate concentration at which v = Vmax/2.

---

### Question 6 (4 points)
Which of the following is TRUE about competitive inhibition?
A. Vmax is decreased
B. Km is increased
C. The inhibitor binds covalently to the enzyme
D. The inhibitor increases the apparent Km but decreases Vmax

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: In competitive inhibition, the inhibitor competes with substrate for the active site. This increases the apparent Km (requiring more substrate to reach half-maximal velocity) but does NOT affect Vmax (which can be achieved with high enough substrate concentration).

---

### Question 7 (4 points)
The fluid mosaic model of cell membranes describes:
A. A rigid structure with proteins fixed in place
B. A dynamic structure with proteins floating in a phospholipid bilayer
C. A membrane composed primarily of cholesterol
D. A single layer of phospholipids

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The Singer-Nicolson Fluid Mosaic Model (1972) proposes that membranes are two-dimensional fluids in which proteins float and diffuse laterally within the lipid bilayer.

---

### Question 8 (4 points)
Which type of transport requires ATP hydrolysis?
A. Simple diffusion
B. Facilitated diffusion
C. Osmosis
D. Primary active transport

**Answer**: D | **Explanation**: Primary active transport (e.g., Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) directly uses ATP hydrolysis to move molecules against their concentration gradients. Passive transport, facilitated diffusion, and osmosis do not require ATP.

### Bioenergetics (Questions 9-12)

### Question 9 (4 points)
The net ATP yield from glycolysis (per glucose molecule) under aerobic conditions is:
A. 2 ATP
B. 4 ATP
C. 30-32 ATP
D. 36-38 ATP

**Answer**: A | **Explanation**: Glycolysis produces 4 ATP but consumes 2 ATP in the early steps, giving a net yield of 2 ATP per glucose. The other ATP come from mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation.

---

### Question 10 (4 points)
The chemiosmotic hypothesis, proposed by Peter Mitchell, states that:
A. ATP is synthesized directly from high-energy intermediates
B. ATP synthesis is driven by a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane
C. Electrons are transferred directly to oxygen
D. The citric acid cycle produces most of the cell's ATP

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Peter Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory (Nobel Prize 1978) proposed that the energy from electron transport creates a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, and ATP synthase uses this gradient to produce ATP.

---

### Question 11 (4 points)
Which enzyme is the rate-limiting step of glycolysis?
A. Hexokinase
B. Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)
C. Pyruvate kinase
D. Aldolase

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: PFK-1 is the major regulatory (rate-limiting) enzyme of glycolysis. It is allosterically regulated by ATP (inhibitor) and AMP/F2,6BP (activators), making it the primary control point.

---

### Question 12 (4 points)
In the electron transport chain, the final electron acceptor is:
A. NAD⁺
B. FAD
C. Oxygen (O₂)
D. Coenzyme Q

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Oxygen (O₂) is the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain. It is reduced to water (H₂O) at Complex IV. Without oxygen, the ETC stops, and ATP production ceases.

### Cell Biology (Questions 13-16)

### Question 13 (4 points)
The resting membrane potential of a typical neuron is approximately:
A. 0 mV
B. -70 mV
C. +30 mV
D. -90 mV

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The resting membrane potential of most neurons is approximately -70 mV (range: -60 to -80 mV). This is primarily determined by K⁺ leak channels (making Vm close to EK = -90 mV) and the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase.

---

### Question 14 (4 points)
Which of the following is NOT a component of the cytoskeleton?
A. Microtubules
B. Actin filaments
C. Intermediate filaments
D. Collagen fibers

**Answer**: D | **Explanation**: Collagen is an extracellular matrix protein, not a component of the cytoskeleton. The cytoskeleton consists of microtubules (α/β-tubulin), actin filaments, and intermediate filaments (vimentin, keratin, etc.).

---

### Question 15 (4 points)
Receptor-mediated endocytosis is important for:
A. Water transport across membranes
B. Nerve impulse transmission
C. Selective uptake of specific molecules like LDL
D. Protein synthesis

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Receptor-mediated endocytosis allows cells to selectively internalize specific ligands via clathrin-coated pits. Examples include LDL uptake (cholesterol) and transferrin uptake (iron).

---

### Question 16 (4 points)
The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase transports:
A. 2 Na⁺ in, 2 K⁺ out per ATP
B. 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in per ATP
C. 1 Na⁺ out, 1 K⁺ in per ATP
D. 3 K⁺ out, 2 Na⁺ in per ATP

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (discovered by Jens Skou, Nobel Prize 1997) moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP hydrolyzed. This creates a net outward current, contributing to the negative resting membrane potential.

### Homeostasis (Questions 17-20)

### Question 17 (4 points)
Homeostasis refers to:
A. The body's response to external environmental changes
B. The maintenance of a stable internal environment despite external changes
C. The production of hormones
D. The breakdown of food molecules

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Homeostasis (from Greek: "homoios" = similar, "stasis" = standing) refers to the body's ability to maintain a relatively stable internal environment despite changes in external conditions. This concept was developed by Walter Cannon (1932).

---

### Question 18 (4 points)
During the absolute refractory period of an action potential:
A. A smaller stimulus can generate an action potential
B. No action potential can be generated regardless of stimulus strength
C. The membrane is more excitable than normal
D. K⁺ channels are opening rapidly

**Answer**: B | **Explanation**: During the absolute refractory period, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are in the inactivated state and cannot be opened. This prevents action potential generation regardless of stimulus strength and ensures unidirectional propagation.

---

### Question 19 (4 points)
Which hormone is released from the adrenal medulla during fight-or-flight response?
A. Cortisol
B. Aldosterone
C. Epinephrine (adrenaline)
D. Insulin

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: The adrenal medulla releases catecholamines (primarily epinephrine 80%, norepinephrine 20%) during sympathetic activation. These hormones increase heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose to prepare the body for immediate action.

---

### Question 20 (4 points)
The hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis is an example of:
A. Direct neural control
B. Positive feedback
C. Negative feedback with hormone cascades
D. Autocrine signaling

**Answer**: C | **Explanation**: The HPT axis operates through a classic hormone cascade with negative feedback. TRH → TSH → Thyroid hormones (T3/T4) → Negative feedback inhibition of both hypothalamus and pituitary. This ensures precise regulation of metabolic rate.

## Part B: Short Answer Questions (20 points)

### Question 1 (4 points)
**Calculate the pH of a buffer solution containing 0.3 M acetic acid (CH₃COOH, pKa = 4.8) and 0.1 M sodium acetate (CH₃COONa). Show your work.**

**Answer**:
```
Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:
pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])

Given:
pKa = 4.8
[HA] = 0.3 M (acetic acid)
[A⁻] = 0.1 M (acetate)

pH = 4.8 + log(0.1/0.3)
pH = 4.8 + log(0.333)
pH = 4.8 + (-0.48)
pH = 4.32

Answer: pH ≈ 4.3
```

---

### Question 2 (4 points)
**Using the Nernst equation, calculate the equilibrium potential for K⁺ given [K⁺]in = 150 mM and [K⁺]out = 5 mM at 37°C.**

**Answer**:
```
Nernst equation:
E = (RT/zF) × ln([X]out/[X]in)

At 37°C (310 K):
RT/F = (8.314 × 310)/96485 = 0.0267 V = 26.7 mV

For K⁺ (z = +1):
E = 26.7 × ln(5/150)
E = 26.7 × ln(0.0333)
E = 26.7 × (-3.40)
E = -90.8 mV

Answer: EK ≈ -91 mV
```

---

### Question 3 (4 points)
**Describe the sequence of events during an action potential, including the roles of Na⁺ and K⁺ channels.**

**Answer**:
```
1. Resting: Vm = -70 mV, Na⁺ channels closed, K⁺ channels mostly closed
2. Threshold: Vm reaches -55 mV, triggering Na⁺ channel opening
3. Depolarization: Na⁺ channels open rapidly, Na⁺ influx causes Vm → +30 mV
4. Peak: Na⁺ channel inactivation begins, Vm peaks
5. Repolarization: K⁺ channels (delayed rectifier) open, K⁺ efflux, Vm → -70 mV
6. Hyperpolarization: K⁺ channels close slowly, Vm briefly below resting
7. Recovery: Na⁺ channels return to closed state, ready for next AP
```

**Compare Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes mellitus, including the primary defect, β-cell status, and typical treatment.**

### Question 5 (4 points)
**Explain why cyanide is lethal. Include the mechanism of action and how it affects cellular respiration.**

**Answer**:
```
Mechanism:
Cyanide (CN⁻) binds to the ferric iron (Fe³⁺) of cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV)
in the electron transport chain.

Effects:
1. Complex IV is inhibited → ETC stops
2. No more proton pumping → No proton gradient
3. No chemiosmotic coupling → ATP synthesis stops
4. NADH cannot be oxidized → [NADH] increases
5. Glycolysis slows (no NAD⁺ regeneration)
6. Cell death due to ATP depletion

Treatment:
- Amyl nitrite/sodium nitrite: Oxidizes Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ (not bound by CN⁻)
- Sodium thiosulfate: Converts CN⁻ to thiocyanate (excreted)
- Hydroxocobalamin: Binds CN⁻ directly
```
