## Multiple Choice (15 questions)

### Section A: Atomic Structure & Bonding

**1. Which element has the electron configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p²?**
- A. Carbon (C)
- B. Silicon (Si)
- C. Nitrogen (N)
- D. Oxygen (O)
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: 1s² + 2s² + 2p⁶ = 10 electrons, + 3s² + 3p² = 14 total. Atomic number 14 = Si. Si is used in semiconductor biosensors (BME application).

---

**2. According to Linus Pauling's electronegativity scale, which bond is most polar?**
- A. C-C
- B. C-H
- C. C-O
- D. Na-Cl
- **Answer**: D | **Explanation**: Δχ(Na-Cl) = 3.16 - 0.93 = 2.23 > 1.7 → Ionic bond. This is the most polar, but technically classified as ionic rather than polar covalent. If considering only covalent bonds, C-O (Δχ = 0.89) would be most polar covalent bond.

---

**3. Which statement about hydrogen bonds is TRUE?**
- A. They are stronger than covalent bonds
- B. They require complete electron transfer
- C. They are responsible for DNA double helix stability
- D. They only occur between water molecules
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Watson & Crick (1953) showed that hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs (A-T: 2 H-bonds, G-C: 3 H-bonds) stabilize the DNA double helix. H-bonds are ~20 kJ/mol, much weaker than covalent bonds (~350 kJ/mol).

---

**4. The pH of human blood is maintained at approximately 7.4. This corresponds to [H⁺] of:**
- A. 3.98 × 10⁻⁸ M
- B. 4.0 × 10⁻⁷ M
- C. 3.98 × 10⁻⁵ M
- D. 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M
- **Answer**: A | **Explanation**: [H⁺] = 10^(-pH) = 10^(-7.4) = 10^(0.6) × 10^(-8) = 3.98 × 10⁻⁸ M. Normal range: 3.98-4.47 × 10⁻⁸ M (pH 7.35-7.45).

---

**5. In the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]), what does [A⁻]/[HA] = 1 indicate?**
- A. Maximum buffer capacity
- B. pH = pKa
- C. The acid is completely dissociated
- D. The solution is strongly acidic
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: When [A⁻] = [HA], log(1) = 0, so pH = pKa. At this point, the buffer has equal amounts of acid and conjugate base. Maximum buffer capacity occurs at pH = pKa ± 1.

### Section B: Water & Biological Systems

**6. Which property of water is MOST important for explaining why ice floats?**
- A. High surface tension
- B. High specific heat
- C. Lower density of solid than liquid
- D. High heat of vaporization
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Water is unique because its solid form (ice) is less dense than liquid water. This is due to the hydrogen-bonded crystalline structure of ice. If ice sank, lakes would freeze from the bottom up, killing aquatic organisms.

---

**7. A molecule with both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions is called:**
- A. Amphoteric
- B. Amphipathic
- C. Anisotropic
- D. Allosteric
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Amphipathic molecules (e.g., phospholipids, bile acids, detergents) have both polar (hydrophilic) and nonpolar (hydrophobic) regions. This property allows formation of micelles and bilayers — essential for cell membranes (Singer-Nicolson 1972 fluid mosaic model).

---

**8. The high specific heat of water (4.18 J/g·K) is important for biological systems because:**
- A. It allows water to dissolve nonpolar substances
- B. It helps organisms maintain stable body temperature
- C. It increases the rate of chemical reactions
- D. It prevents water from evaporating
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: High specific heat means water absorbs/releases large amounts of heat with minimal temperature change. This helps mammals maintain homeostasis (~37°C) and prevents rapid temperature fluctuations in aquatic environments.

### Section C: Functional Groups

**9. Which functional group is found in amino acids that allows them to act as both acids and bases?**
- A. -OH only
- B. -COOH and -NH₂
- C. -SH only
- D. -PO₄²⁻ only
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Amino acids contain a carboxyl group (-COOH) that can donate H⁺ (acid) and an amino group (-NH₂) that can accept H⁺ (base). At physiological pH, they exist as zwitterions: ⁺H₃N-CH(R)-COO⁻.

---

**10. The peptide bond that links amino acids in proteins is an example of:**
- A. Ester bond
- B. Hydrogen bond
- C. Amide bond
- D. Disulfide bond
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: The peptide bond is a covalent amide bond formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another (R-C(=O)-NH-R). It has partial double-bond character (resonance), making it planar and relatively rigid.

---

**11. Disulfide bridges in proteins are formed between:**
- A. Two -OH groups
- B. Two -SH groups
- C. -COOH and -NH₂
- D. Phosphate groups
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Disulfide bonds form by oxidation of two cysteine sulfhydryl groups (-SH): 2 -SH → -S-S- + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻. These covalent bonds stabilize protein tertiary and quaternary structure. Anfinden (1957) showed that disulfide bonds are not required for primary sequence but affect folding.

### Section D: Clinical Applications

**12. A patient with COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) has blood pH = 7.32, PCO₂ = 55 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ = 30 mM. This represents:**
- A. Metabolic acidosis
- B. Respiratory acidosis with metabolic compensation
- C. Respiratory alkalosis
- D. Normal blood gases
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Acidemia (pH < 7.35) with elevated PCO₂ (> 45 mmHg) indicates respiratory acidosis. The elevated HCO₃⁻ (24 mM normal) shows kidney compensation. In chronic respiratory acidosis, HCO₃⁻ increases ~3.5 mM per 10 mmHg ↑PCO₂. Expected: 24 + (15/10)×3.5 = 29.25 mM ≈ 30 mM.

**13. Which of the following drugs would MOST likely have poor oral bioavailability?**
- A. MW = 250 Da, LogP = 2, H-donors = 2
- B. MW = 800 Da, LogP = 6, H-donors = 3
- C. MW = 400 Da, LogP = 3, H-donors = 4
- D. MW = 300 Da, LogP = 1, H-donors = 1
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Lipinski's Rule of 5 violations: MW > 500 Da (800 > 500) and LogP > 5 (6 > 5). High MW and lipophilicity reduce oral bioavailability. The molecule cannot be absorbed through intestinal epithelium.

---

**14. A buffer prepared with equal concentrations of acetic acid (CH₃COOH) and sodium acetate (CH₃COONa) will have pH approximately:**
- A. 2.37
- B. 4.74
- C. 7.00
- D. 9.25
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Using Henderson-Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]) = 4.74 + log(1) = 4.74. The pKa of acetic acid is 4.74. When [A⁻] = [HA], pH = pKa by definition.

---

**15. In an acid-base titration of a weak acid with a strong base, the pH at the equivalence point is:**
- A. Less than 7
- B. Equal to 7
- C. Greater than 7
- D. Cannot be determined
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: At equivalence, all weak acid has been converted to its conjugate base (acetate). The acetate ion hydrolyzes: CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻, producing a basic solution. pH > 7 (for 0.1 M acetate, pH ≈ 8.7).

## Short Answer Questions (5 questions)

### Question 1 (8 marks)
**Explain, using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, why the bicarbonate buffer system (CO₂/HCO₃⁻) is effective at maintaining blood pH despite continuous addition of metabolic acids (CO₂ from cellular respiration).**

**Model Answer**:
The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for the bicarbonate system is:
```
pH = pKa + log([HCO₃⁻]/[CO₂])
pH = 6.1 + log([HCO₃⁻]/[CO₂])
```

Key points:
1. **Physiological pKa**: The pKa of CO₂/HCO₃⁻ in blood (6.1) is close to normal blood pH (7.4), providing optimal buffering near physiological conditions.

2. **Respiratory component**: CO₂ is in equilibrium with dissolved CO₂ (PCO₂ × 0.0301 mM/mmHg). The lungs rapidly eliminate excess CO₂ by adjusting ventilation (hyperventilation reduces PCO₂, raising pH).

3. **Metabolic component**: HCO₃⁻ concentration is regulated by the kidneys (reabsorption, H⁺ secretion, NH₄⁺ excretion).

4. **Buffer capacity**: Normal blood [HCO₃⁻] ≈ 24 mM, [CO₂] ≈ 1.2 mM. The ratio [HCO₃⁻]/[CO₂] = 20, giving:
   pH = 6.1 + log(20) = 6.1 + 1.3 = 7.4 ✓

5. **Compensation**: When metabolic acids produce excess H⁺:
   H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → H₂CO₃ → CO₂ + H₂O
   The CO₂ is exhaled, preventing accumulation.

### Question 2 (8 marks)
**Compare the four types of chemical bonds/interactions found in biological systems (covalent, ionic, hydrogen, and hydrophobic) in terms of: (a) bond strength, (b) directionality, and (c) biological significance.**

**Model Answer**:

| Bond Type | Strength (kJ/mol) | Directionality | Biological Significance |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|------------------------|
| **Covalent** | 200-1100 | High (specific geometry) | Peptide bonds, DNA backbone, primary structure |
| **Ionic** | 400-4000 (in vacuum) | None (electrostatic) | Salt bridges, ion channels, membrane potential |
| **Hydrogen** | 10-40 | Moderate (angular) | DNA base pairing, protein folding, enzyme-substrate binding |
| **Hydrophobic** | Entropic | None | Membrane formation, protein folding (core), drug-receptor interactions |

Key differences:
- **Covalent bonds** are permanent and strong; breaking them requires significant energy
- **Ionic bonds** in biological systems are weakened by water (dielectric constant ε = 78.5)
- **Hydrogen bonds** are individually weak but collectively provide specificity
- **Hydrophobic effect** is actually entropically driven (not a bond per se) — nonpolar groups cluster to minimize disruption of water hydrogen-bond network

### Question 3 (8 marks)
**A patient presents with metabolic acidosis. The blood gas shows: pH = 7.20, PCO₂ = 30 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ = 12 mM.**

**(a) Is this primary metabolic acidosis or respiratory acidosis? Justify your answer.**
**(b) Calculate the expected PCO₂ using Winter's formula. Is the respiratory compensation appropriate?**
**(c) Suggest two possible causes of this acid-base disturbance.**

**Model Answer**:

**(a)** Primary metabolic acidosis:
- pH = 7.20 < 7.35 (acidemia)
- HCO₃⁻ = 12 mM < 22 mM (low) ← indicates metabolic problem
- PCO₂ = 30 mmHg < 35 mmHg (low) ← respiratory compensation

**(b)** Winter's Formula for expected PCO₂:
```
PCO₂ expected = 1.5 × [HCO₃⁻] + 8 ± 2
               = 1.5 × 12 + 8
               = 18 + 8
               = 26 mmHg (range: 24-28 mmHg)
```
Actual PCO₂ = 30 mmHg, which is slightly higher than expected (compensation is slightly inadequate or there's concurrent respiratory issue).

**(c)** Possible causes of metabolic acidosis:
1. **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)** — Accumulation of ketone bodies (β-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate)
2. **Lactic acidosis** — Tissue hypoxia → anaerobic glycolysis → lactate accumulation
3. **Renal failure** — Inability to excrete H⁺ or regenerate HCO₃⁻
4. **Severe diarrhea** — Loss of intestinal bicarbonate
5. **Methanol/ethylene glycol poisoning** — Accumulation of organic acids

### Question 4 (8 marks)
**Using your knowledge of electronegativity and molecular structure, explain why:**
**(a) Methanol (CH₃OH) is miscible with water, but methane (CH₄) is not.**
**(b) Carbon is uniquely suited to form the backbone of biological macromolecules.**

**Model Answer**:

**(a)** Miscibility differences:
- **Methanol**: Has a polar O-H bond (Δχ = 1.24). The -OH group can form hydrogen bonds with water molecules. This allows methanol to dissolve in water at any ratio (miscible).
- **Methane**: Only has C-H bonds (Δχ = 0.35), which are nonpolar. No atom capable of H-bonding. Cannot interact favorably with water molecules. When placed in water, methane molecules cluster together (hydrophobic effect), so it separates as a distinct phase (immiscible).

**(b)** Carbon's unique properties:
1. **Tetravalence**: Can form 4 covalent bonds (C: 1s² 2s² 2p² → 4 unpaired electrons)
2. **Bond diversity**: Forms strong bonds with H, O, N, P, S, and itself
3. **Bond strength**: C-C (348 kJ/mol), C-H (413 kJ/mol) — strong enough for stability, weak enough for metabolism
4. **Versatility**: Single, double, triple bonds; linear, branched, cyclic structures
5. **Size**: Atomic radius适中 (77 pm) — large enough for complexity, small enough for density
6. **Catena-forming**: Can form long chains, rings, and complex 3D structures

These properties allow carbon to form the diverse molecules of life: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.

### Question 5 (8 marks)
**Design a phosphate buffer for an experiment requiring pH 7.2. The total phosphate concentration should be 0.1 M.**

**(a) Calculate the ratio [HPO₄²⁻]/[H₂PO₄⁻] needed.**
**(b) Calculate the individual concentrations of each species.**
**(c) Calculate how many grams of Na₂HPO₄ (FW = 141.96) and NaH₂PO₄ (FW = 119.98) are needed to prepare 500 mL of this buffer.**
**(d) What is the buffer capacity of this system?**

**Model Answer**:

**(a)** Ratio calculation:
```
pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
7.2 = 7.21 + log([HPO₄²⁻]/[H₂PO₄⁻])
log([HPO₄²⁻]/[H₂PO₄⁻]) = 7.2 - 7.21 = -0.01
[HPO₄²⁻]/[H₂PO₄⁻] = 10^(-0.01) = 0.977
```

**(b)** Individual concentrations:
```
Let x = [H₂PO₄⁻], then [HPO₄²⁻] = 0.977x
x + 0.977x = 0.1 M
1.977x = 0.1
x = 0.0506 M = [H₂PO₄⁻]
[HPO₄²⁻] = 0.0506 × 0.977 = 0.0494 M
```

**(c)** Mass calculations for 500 mL:
```
Moles needed:
n(Na₂HPO₄) = 0.0494 M × 0.5 L = 0.0247 mol
n(NaH₂PO₄) = 0.0506 M × 0.5 L = 0.0253 mol

Mass needed:
Mass(Na₂HPO₄) = 0.0247 mol × 141.96 g/mol = 3.51 g
Mass(NaH₂PO₄) = 0.0253 mol × 119.98 g/mol = 3.04 g
```

**(d)** Buffer capacity:
```
β = 2.303 × C_total × Ka × [H⁺] / (Ka + [H⁺])²

Ka = 10^(-7.21) = 6.17 × 10⁻⁸
[H⁺] = 10^(-7.2) = 6.31 × 10⁻⁸ M
C_total = 0.1 M

β = 2.303 × 0.1 × (6.17×10⁻⁸) × (6.31×10⁻⁸) / [(6.17×10⁻⁸) + (6.31×10⁻⁸)]²
  = 2.303 × 0.1 × (3.89×10⁻¹⁵) / (1.248×10⁻¹⁴)²
  = 0.089 mol/L per pH unit
```
