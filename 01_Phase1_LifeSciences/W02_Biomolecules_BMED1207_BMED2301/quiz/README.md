# Week 2 Self-Quiz — Biomolecules & Biochemistry

## Multiple Choice (15 questions)

**1. Which amino acid is classified as a helix breaker in protein secondary structure?**
- A. Alanine
- B. Leucine
- C. Proline
- D. Isoleucine
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Proline has a cyclic side chain that restricts backbone dihedral angles and lacks an amide hydrogen for hydrogen bonding, making it a strong helix breaker.

**2. In Michaelis-Menten kinetics, what does a Km value represent?**
- A. The substrate concentration at maximum velocity
- B. The substrate concentration at which v = Vmax/2
- C. The turnover number of the enzyme
- D. The catalytic efficiency
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Km is the Michaelis constant, defined as the substrate concentration at which reaction velocity is half-maximal. It equals (k₋₁ + k₂)/k₁.

**3. Which type of inhibition results in unchanged Vmax but increased Km?**
- A. Non-competitive inhibition
- B. Competitive inhibition
- C. Uncompetitive inhibition
- D. Mixed inhibition
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Competitive inhibitors bind to the active site, competing with substrate. More substrate can overcome the inhibition, so Vmax is unchanged but Km (apparent) increases.

**4. DNA and RNA differ in that DNA contains:**
- A. Uracil instead of thymine
- B. Deoxyribose instead of ribose
- C. A single phosphate group
- D. Only purine bases
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: DNA contains deoxyribose (lacks 2'-OH), while RNA contains ribose. This makes DNA more stable and suitable for information storage.

**5. Which statement about the fluid mosaic model is correct?**
- A. Membrane proteins do not move laterally
- B. Lipids are the only component of the membrane
- C. Membrane is a dynamic structure with lateral protein movement
- D. Carbohydrates are covalently attached to lipids only
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Singer & Nicolson (1972) proposed that membranes are fluid (lipids and proteins can diffuse laterally) and mosaic (diverse protein components).

**6. The peptide bond is classified as:**
- A. An ester bond
- B. A disulfide bond
- C. An amide bond
- D. A hydrogen bond
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: The peptide bond is a covalent amide bond between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another.

**7. Which of the following is a reducing sugar?**
- A. Sucrose
- B. Maltose
- C. Lactose
- D. Trehalose
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: Maltose has a free anomeric carbon (reducing end) because it has an α1-4 linkage that leaves one glucose's anomeric carbon free. Sucrose has an α1-2β linkage with both anomeric carbons involved in the glycosidic bond.

**8. In competitive inhibition, what happens to the Lineweaver-Burk plot?**
- A. Same y-intercept, different x-intercept
- B. Same x-intercept, different y-intercept
- C. Both intercepts change
- D. Lines become parallel
- **Answer**: A | **Explanation**: Competitive inhibition: 1/Vmax unchanged (same y-intercept), Km increases (x-intercept = -1/Km moves right, closer to origin).

**9. The quaternary structure of a protein refers to:**
- A. The linear sequence of amino acids
- B. The 3D arrangement of a single polypeptide
- C. The assembly of multiple polypeptide subunits
- D. The local folding patterns
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Quaternary structure describes how multiple polypeptide chains (subunits) assemble to form a functional protein complex.

**10. Cholesterol in animal cell membranes:**
- A. Increases membrane fluidity at low temperatures
- B. Decreases membrane fluidity at high temperatures
- C. Both A and B
- D. Makes membranes completely rigid
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Cholesterol has dual effects: It prevents fatty acid chains from packing too tightly (increases fluidity at low T) and prevents excessive movement (decreases fluidity at high T).

**11. Which amino acid is positively charged at physiological pH (7.4)?**
- A. Aspartate
- B. Glutamate
- C. Lysine
- D. Serine
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Lysine has a side chain pKa of 10.5, so at pH 7.4 it is protonated (+1 charge). Aspartate and glutamate are negatively charged; serine is neutral.

**12. The active site of an enzyme typically:**
- A. Is composed of only one amino acid
- B. Is a rigid, unchanging structure
- C. Occupies 10-20% of the protein volume
- D. Comprises residues that may be distant in sequence but close in 3D
- **Answer**: D | **Explanation**: Active sites often consist of residues brought together by protein folding from different parts of the primary sequence (conformational proximity).

**13. Which of the following is NOT a function of carbohydrates?**
- A. Energy storage
- B. Structural support
- C. Genetic information storage
- D. Cell recognition
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Carbohydrates are involved in energy (glycogen, starch), structure (cellulose, chitin), and recognition (glycoproteins). Genetic information is stored in nucleic acids.

**14. Hemoglobin vs. Myoglobin:**
- A. Hemoglobin has higher affinity for oxygen
- B. Myoglobin has 4 subunits
- C. Hemoglobin shows cooperative oxygen binding
- D. Both are monomers
- **Answer**: C | **Explanation**: Hemoglobin (2α2β tetramer) shows cooperative, sigmoid O₂ binding, while myoglobin (single subunit) has hyperbolic binding.

**15. A protein with high GRAVY (grand average of hydropathicity) score is likely:**
- A. A secreted protein
- B. An integral membrane protein
- C. A cytoplasmic enzyme
- D. A nuclear protein
- **Answer**: B | **Explanation**: High GRAVY indicates hydrophobic character, typical of integral membrane proteins that span the lipid bilayer.

## Short Answer Questions

### Question 1 (10 marks)
**Explain the difference between competitive and non-competitive inhibition. Include effects on Vmax, Km, and Lineweaver-Burk plots.**

**Model Answer**:

| Property | Competitive | Non-competitive |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Binding site | Active site | Allosteric site |
| Vmax | Unchanged | Decreased |
| Km | Increased | Unchanged |
| LB plot y-intercept | Unchanged | Increased |
| LB plot x-intercept | Changes | Unchanged |

**Mechanism**:
- Competitive: Inhibitor resembles substrate, competes for active site. Increasing [S] can overcome inhibition. Effect can be overcome by substrate excess.
- Non-competitive: Inhibitor binds to enzyme (or ES complex) at site distinct from active site. Cannot be overcome by substrate excess. May cause conformational change that reduces catalytic efficiency.

### Question 2 (10 marks)
**Describe the four levels of protein structure and explain how mutations can affect each level.**

**Model Answer**:

1. **Primary structure**: Linear amino acid sequence. Mutations can cause:
   - Silent mutation (no change)
   - Missense mutation (different amino acid)
   - Nonsense mutation (premature stop)

2. **Secondary structure**: α-helix, β-sheet patterns stabilized by H-bonds. Mutations can disrupt helix formers/breakers (e.g., Pro in helix).

3. **Tertiary structure**: 3D fold of single polypeptide. Mutations can:
   - Disrupt hydrophobic core
   - Alter disulfide bonds (Cys mutations)
   - Affect active site geometry

4. **Quaternary structure**: Multi-subunit assembly. Mutations can:
   - Prevent subunit association
   - Cause incorrect subunit pairing

### Question 3 (10 marks)
**A patient has elevated serum CK (creatine kinase) and LDH levels after chest pain. What does this suggest clinically?**

**Model Answer**:
- CK and LDH are intracellular enzymes released when cells are damaged
- Elevated CK-MB isoform (specific for cardiac muscle) suggests myocardial infarction
- LDH elevation (LDH1 > LDH2 pattern) also supports cardiac damage
- Peak CK-MB occurs 12-24 hours post-MI
- Troponin would be the most specific marker (troponin I or T)
- Need to correlate with ECG and clinical presentation
